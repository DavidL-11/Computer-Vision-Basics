8-point algorithm

The fundamental matrix \(F\) can be estimated from matching points alone, without knowing anything about the cameras. Every correspondence gives one linear equation, and eight of them determine \(F\). Add noise, turn normalization and the rank-2 constraint on and off, and compare the estimated epipolar lines with the true ones.

One equation per correspondence

Corresponding points \(x_0 = (u_0, v_0, 1)^\top\) and \(x_1 = (u_1, v_1, 1)^\top\) satisfy the epipolar constraint \(x_1^\top F x_0 = 0\). Written out, this is a linear equation in the nine entries of \(F\):

$$ u_1 u_0 f_{11} + u_1 v_0 f_{12} + u_1 f_{13} + v_1 u_0 f_{21} + v_1 v_0 f_{22} + v_1 f_{23} + u_0 f_{31} + v_0 f_{32} + f_{33} = 0 $$

Each of the \(n\) correspondences adds one row to a homogeneous system:

$$ \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} u_1 u_0 & u_1 v_0 & u_1 & v_1 u_0 & v_1 v_0 & v_1 & u_0 & v_0 & 1 \\ \vdots & & & & & & & & \vdots \end{bmatrix}}_{A \;(n \times 9)} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} f_{11} \\ f_{12} \\ \vdots \\ f_{33} \end{bmatrix}}_{f} = 0 $$

\(F\) has 9 entries but is only defined up to scale, which leaves 8 unknowns. So at least 8 correspondences are needed, with linearly independent rows. With more points and noise there is no exact solution, and the unit vector \(f\) that makes \(|Af|\) smallest is the right singular vector of the smallest singular value, the last column of \(V\) in the SVD \(A = U \Sigma V^\top\). This is the same step as for a homography and for the projection matrix in DLT calibration.

Normalize the coordinates first

In pixel coordinates the columns of \(A\) have very different sizes: \(u_1 u_0\) is around \(10^5\), while the last column is 1. The least-squares solution minimizes an algebraic error in which the large terms dominate, and the small singular values of \(A\) that decide the solution get lost next to the large ones. The result is very sensitive to noise.

The fix is to change the coordinate system of each image before building \(A\): move the origin to the centroid of the points and scale them so that their mean distance from it is \(\sqrt 2\). Coordinates are then of the order of 1:

$$ \hat x_0 = T_0 x_0, \quad \hat x_1 = T_1 x_1, \qquad T = \begin{bmatrix} s & 0 & -s\,\bar u \\ 0 & s & -s\,\bar v \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad s = \frac{\sqrt 2}{\text{mean distance from } (\bar u, \bar v)} $$

The 8-point algorithm on \(\hat x_0, \hat x_1\) gives \(\hat F\). Since \(\hat x_1^\top \hat F \hat x_0 = x_1^\top T_1^\top \hat F T_0 x_0\), the matrix for the pixel coordinates is \(F = T_1^\top \hat F T_0\).

Enforce rank 2

A true fundamental matrix is singular, it has rank 2, and all epipolar lines of an image meet in the epipole. The solution of \(Af = 0\) does not know this, and with noise it comes out with full rank: its epipolar lines no longer pass through a common point. The repair takes the SVD of the estimate, sets the smallest singular value to zero and multiplies it back together:

$$ \hat F = U \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3) V^\top \;\rightarrow\; U \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, 0) V^\top $$

This is the rank-2 matrix closest to the estimate in the Frobenius norm, the rank approximation of the SVD. It is applied to \(\hat F\) before the normalization is undone.

Algebraic and geometric error

The 8-point algorithm minimizes \(|Af|\), an algebraic quantity without a direct meaning in the image. The panel measures the error that matters: the distance in pixels of each \(x_1\) from its epipolar line \(\hat F x_0\), for the points used and for other points that played no part in the estimate. Like linear calibration, the 8-point algorithm is fast and needs no initial guess, and its result is usually refined by a non-linear method that minimizes a geometric error.

Try this

  • Set the noise to 0 and the points to 8: \(\sigma_9 = 0\), \(\hat F\) equals \(F\), and the estimated lines cover the true ones.
  • Set the noise back to 1 px and turn normalization off. The column sizes of \(A\) range over five orders of magnitude, \(\sigma_1 / \sigma_8\) jumps, and the error on the other points grows several times.
  • Without normalization, compare enforce rank 2 on and off: forcing rank 2 in pixel coordinates makes the error even larger. With normalization it hardly costs anything.
  • Turn off enforce rank 2 and zoom out until you reach an epipole: the epipolar lines no longer meet in one point but cross in a cloud. The panel shows \(\sigma_3 \ne 0\).
  • Increase the number of points with 1 px noise: the error on the other points shrinks, while the error on the used points stays near the noise level.
  • Press New noise a few times with only 8 points: the estimated epipoles jump around. Exactly determined systems fit the noise perfectly, and more points average it out.
